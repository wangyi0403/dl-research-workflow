# Pre-Submission Checklists

Select checks for the actual article type, submission stage and confirmed venue. Mark irrelevant items not applicable. A checklist omission is not a scientific defect until its consequence or applicable requirement is established.

## Source and Presentation

- The actual submission source compiles, and affected rendered pages have been inspected.
- Layout warnings are assessed for visible clipping, overflow or unreadability rather than treated as automatic blockers.
- The current venue's template, page/word limits and rules about counting references or appendices are verified.
- Unresolved placeholders are identified; citations, cross-references and intended figure/table references resolve.
- Notation, units, terminology and meaningful numerical precision are consistent.
- Captions, uncertainty definitions, legends and table notes make the evidence interpretable at manuscript size.
- Table rules, caption position, typography and bibliography inclusion follow the confirmed venue or established project style. Do not require booktabs, ban vertical rules or remove uncited bibliography entries as universal rules.

## Argument and Evidence

- The abstract conveys the question, approach, supported finding and scope appropriate to the article type. Enforce structured headings or a numerical result only when applicable.
- The introduction makes the actual contribution identifiable without an arbitrary paragraph count, citation quota or required vocabulary.
- Related work fairly establishes the relevant comparisons and evidence gap. Thematic, chronological or other organization is acceptable when it serves the argument.
- Methods, results and conclusion agree on the claims, data scope and evidence strength.
- Discussion interprets the results without inventing a causal explanation. Connect relevant prior work where it changes the interpretation.
- Real limitations are visible where needed; a dedicated section or future-work paragraph is required only when appropriate to the venue or article.
- Ambiguous or difficult prose is assessed in context rather than flagged by sentence length or a fixed phrase alone.

## Study-Specific Checks

### Theory and Conceptual Work

- Core concepts, assumptions and logical steps are sufficiently defined.
- When theoretical novelty is claimed, its relationship to existing theory and its supported increment are explicit.
- Empirical, methodological or benchmark work is judged against its actual contribution; absence of new theory is not a defect by itself.

### Empirical and Computational Work

- The design, units of analysis, data boundaries and comparisons can answer the stated question.
- Baselines and evaluation budgets are fair and relevant; novelty is not established solely by a current leaderboard ranking.
- Ablations or other discriminating tests are used where needed to support a component or mechanism claim, rather than required for every study type.
- Reported values and uncertainty are traceable to recorded results, aggregation code and configurations.
- Statistical inference, when used, matches dependence, sampling and the intended claim. Report estimation precision and practical magnitude when material; do not require significance testing for every benchmark or descriptive result.

### Qualitative and Mixed Methods

- The sampling rationale and analysis procedure fit the selected methodological approach.
- Coding, trustworthiness, saturation and reflexivity are assessed when relevant to that approach; no one technique is universally mandatory.
- Mixed-method claims explain how the evidence sources are integrated.

## Submission Requirements to Verify

Use the actual venue, year, track, article type and submission stage. Record the official source and verification date; historical presets are discovery hints, not current rules.

| Venue family or artifact | Requirements to look up when applicable |
|---|---|
| NeurIPS / ICLR / ICML | Current template and length limits; anonymity; checklist, impact or reproducibility statements; supplementary/code policies; accepted-paper versus review-stage requirements |
| IEEE | Specific journal/conference template; abstract/keyword limits; reference style; figure resolution and caption rules; pseudocode environment compatibility |
| ACM | Applicable class/options, anonymity stage, CCS/reference-format and rights requirements |
| Thesis | Current university template, required language sections/declarations and bibliography standard/version |
| Other journal or venue | Article type, source/package requirements, disclosures, author declarations, data/code availability and supplementary limits |

Only report a compliance blocker against a verified applicable requirement. Missing policy verification remains unknown, and a completed script screen does not establish scientific readiness.
