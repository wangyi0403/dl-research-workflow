# Author stance and defensive writing

Use when a passage retreats from its supported judgment, repeats generic
limitations, anticipates a stronger claim nobody made, or imports audit language
into manuscript prose. Preserve uncertainty, negative results and limitations
when they carry scientific meaning.

## Recover the author's judgment

From the manuscript, nearby results and accepted decisions, identify the
intended reader judgment, its supplied support, the necessary inference and any
condition that changes its interpretation. Keep this model internal. Ask only
when an unresolved scientific choice changes the passage and cannot be recovered.

## Calibrate the proposition

- State its object, comparison, conditions and modality directly. Avoid a broad
  assertion followed by its withdrawal.
- Keep an already tentative explanation tentative. Do not turn it into
  necessary proof and then warn that the evidence cannot prove it.
- Preserve direct conclusions supported by the evidence and accepted position.
  Insert `merely`, `preliminary`, `we attempt`, or `cannot establish` only when
  they state real status or scope, not automatic editorial self-doubt.
- Let one qualifier carry one uncertainty. Distinct uncertainties may need
  distinct conditions; do not erase them to make the prose confident.
- For each added caveat, identify its evidence and the misunderstanding its
  omission would cause. If neither can be identified, omit the caveat.
- Discuss alternatives when they account for the observations and change the
  interpretation, rather than listing every imaginable possibility. Keep open
  scientific choices outside final prose or explicitly provisional when a
  provisional draft is requested.

## Preserve the paragraph's scientific work

After deleting, merging or rewriting, reread the affected paragraph and its
handoff. Its judgment, evidence, necessary inference, material boundaries,
comparisons and citation/figure/equation references must survive. Repair a
missing relationship rather than inserting an empty transition or ending every
paragraph with another caveat.

Internal verification notes, tool failures, rejected drafts and review history
stay in the collaboration or project records unless they affect reproducibility
or scientific interpretation. Deliver manuscript-ready text; an audit request
may receive the material findings separately.

## Constructed examples

Each example's premise is supplied here, not evidence for a real manuscript.

1. **Measured improvement.** With a controlled comparison showing a 12% error
   reduction on the specified test set, replace `The method may offer
   preliminary potential, but this does not prove universal superiority` with
   `The method reduced error by 12% relative to the baseline on the test set.`
   Preserve the number and test scope. No universal superiority was claimed.
2. **Possible mechanism.** With an accepted interpretation that a phase shift
   may explain a positive bias, write `The phase shift may explain the positive
   bias.` Do not recast it as necessity and deny proof. If an actual alternative
   changes the interpretation, discuss that alternative and its evidence.
3. **Material boundary.** With evaluation at one site and no external validation,
   retain `Performance across sites remains untested.` Compress apologies,
   not this independent limitation. Do not claim general applicability.
4. **Association.** With observational association only, preserve `X was
   associated with Y.` Changing this to `X caused Y` changes the science.
5. **Hypothesis.** Preserve the status of an untested proposed explanation.
   Unverified citation support is an editorial check, not evidence it is false.
6. **Missing material.** Mark a missing value or request it outside the prose.
   Do not invent it or declare all study results unreliable.

The finish criterion is clear, evidence-calibrated author expression. Detector
scores, rigid word bans and stronger-looking language do not establish it.
