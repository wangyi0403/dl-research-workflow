# Socratic Question Bank for Paper Planning

Use these questions before writing the outline. Force clarity at the idea level before committing to a structure.

## Core Questions (Always Ask)

1. **"What is the ONE thing you want readers to remember after reading this paper?"**
   If you can't answer in one sentence, the paper has no center.

2. **"If a reviewer only reads Fig 1 and the abstract, what should they understand?"**
   The paper's takeaway must survive a 30-second skim.

3. **"What's the essential difference between your method and the strongest baseline — beyond 'we used attention'?"**
   Distinguish mechanism from buzzword. If the difference is just a component name, it's not a difference.

4. **"What question are you most afraid a reviewer will ask, that you haven't fully addressed?"**
   Confront the weakest point before the reviewer does.

5. **"If this paper could only have ONE contribution, what would it be?"**
   Prioritize. Everything else is supporting evidence.

## Structure Questions

6. **"Why does Related Work come before/after Method?"**
   Some venues expect RW before Method (context first); others want Method first (novelty first). Know your venue.

7. **"Does the Running Example appear in Introduction, Method, AND Results?"**
   If it disappears after the intro, it's not a running example — it's decoration.

8. **"Is each figure referenced at least twice in the text?"**
   Once to introduce it ("Fig 3 shows...") and once to discuss what it means ("As Fig 3 demonstrates...").

## Evidence Questions

9. **"For every claim in the contribution list, which specific table/figure proves it?"**
   No orphan claims. Every claim has a home in results.

10. **"What's the weakest result in the paper, and why should the reader still believe the overall story?"**
    Acknowledge weakness; explain why it doesn't undermine the main finding.

11. **"If you removed the ablation study, would the main comparison still be convincing?"**
    If yes, the ablations are too weak. If no, they're doing their job.

## Venue-Specific Questions

12. **"Why this journal and not [top competitor journal]?"**
    Must have a real answer beyond "it's good enough for us."

13. **"Have you cited at least 3 papers from this journal published in the last 2 years?"**
    Shows you know the community you're submitting to.

14. **"Does the abstract mention a topic that's in the journal's Aims & Scope?"**
    Editors desk-reject papers that don't match scope. Make the match explicit.

## Narrative Questions

15. **"What's the story arc?"**
    Problem → Why it's hard → What we did → What happened → What it means. Not: "We did X. Then we did Y. Table 1 shows Z."

16. **"If a non-expert reads only the first sentence of each paragraph, can they follow the argument?"**
    Topic sentences should form a coherent summary.

17. **"Where does the reader feel surprised?"**
    A paper without surprises is a paper without insight. Where's the counterintuitive finding?

## Kill Criteria

If answer to any of these is "no," reconsider the outline:

- [ ] Can I state the main contribution in <25 words?
- [ ] Does every section have a clear purpose that differs from every other section?
- [ ] Is there a figure/table for every major claim?
- [ ] Does the conclusion match the introduction's promise?
