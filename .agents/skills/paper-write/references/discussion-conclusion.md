# Discussion and Conclusion

Interpret the completed evidence and answer the research question at its warranted strength. Use the accepted Claim state and analysis, not a remembered aspiration or a fixed closing template.

Explain what the primary result changes, how it relates to the closest evidence, and which proposed explanation is supported. Distinguish association, contribution of a design choice, an identified mechanism and a formal guarantee. Discuss a plausible alternative when it changes the interpretation and has a concrete basis, not because every possible alternative must be listed.

Treat mixed evidence according to its consequence. A material contradiction requires revising the claim or resolving the evidence. A measured trade-off can define a useful operating regime. A secondary result need not become the paper's global verdict, but its placement must not hide information needed to interpret the central claim.

State real limitations through the affected claim and their impact. Internal, external, measurement, statistical and operational boundaries differ; preserve the ones that matter. Include a next test when supplied or requested and when it can discriminate between outcomes. Do not invent future work to complete a rhetorical pattern.

The conclusion gives the supported answer, the decisive insight and its scientific or practical implication. Its scope agrees with the title, abstract and introduction. Applications distinguish demonstrated use from expected use. Do not introduce an unverified result, new causal explanation or deployment promise in the closing paragraph.
