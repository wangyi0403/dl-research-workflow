# Evidence-Led Academic Writing

Use these principles after the paper plan, results, and figure/table evidence are ready. They guide decisions; the target venue, study design, and author instructions determine the final form.

## Evidence before prose

Write from a claim/evidence/figure map rather than from a blank page or remembered results.

- Each central claim has a permissible wording, source artifact, and known limitation.
- Figures, tables, and analysis code are checked before the prose treats them as findings.
- Observation, statistical result, interpretation, mechanism explanation, and speculation remain distinguishable.
- An unresolved source stays visibly unresolved; fluent prose does not close an evidence gap.

## Contribution and implementation

A new method, system, dataset, workflow, empirical finding, negative result, benchmark, or design principle may constitute a contribution when it changes what the target community can know or do. Do not require every paper to claim a universal principle, and do not present an artifact or score as a knowledge contribution without the supporting argument.

Useful self-check:

> What precise capability, finding, explanation, resource, or decision-relevant knowledge does this paper add, for whom, under what tested conditions?

The answer must match the actual evidence and target venue. If the evidence supports only a scoped empirical finding, write that rather than elevating it into a law or paradigm.

## Narrative construction

Build the narrative from reader questions:

1. What consequential problem or evidence gap exists?
2. What do the closest approaches establish, and where do they stop?
3. What claim does this study test?
4. How do the method and study design test that claim?
5. What did the evidence show, fail to show, or leave uncertain?
6. What is the resulting scope of use or knowledge?

Section order and labels depend on the paper type and venue. A method paper, empirical study, benchmark, review, negative-result paper, and engineering case study need not share one fixed outline.

## Paragraph modules

Recover the author's intended reader judgment, its support and the necessary inference before drafting. Keep this working model internal. Drafting expresses the current scientific position; auditing tests it and returns findings for integration. A request to write does not ask for an audit report embedded in manuscript prose.

Each paragraph should have a visible job. A useful module is:

- topic or local claim;
- evidence, method detail, or comparison;
- interpretation bounded by that evidence;
- transition when the next paragraph depends on it.

This is not a mandatory sentence template. Vary length and structure to fit the argument while keeping terminology and referents stable.

## Related work

Synthesize rather than enumerate. Compare the literature by research question, object/data, method or assumption, principal finding, evidence depth, and boundary. Preserve disagreements and version relationships. The closest work receives the most precise comparison.

Do not manufacture a gap by omitting relevant work, treating a search miss as novelty, or describing every prior method through the limitation your method happens to address.

## Results and discussion

- Report decisive comparisons, uncertainty, failures, and protocol deviations.
- Explain what changed relative to the preregistered or planned claim.
- Use isolation tests, ablations, counterfactuals, or alternatives only when they discriminate between plausible explanations.
- Do not interpret a bug, leak, template shortcut, or unstable result as scientific insight.

## Limitations and conclusion

Calibrate the proposition itself: its object, comparison, conditions and actual certainty. Do not assert a broad claim and then undo it with a disclaimer. A possible explanation remains possible; it need not become a proof claim followed by a denial. Express one uncertainty once, and preserve supported assertive wording as well as warranted hedging.

Add a caveat when omitting it would change the measurement, interpretation, applicable scope or warranted strength. Explain an actual limitation through its constraint and effect; include a next validation only when supplied or requested. Do not require every paragraph or conclusion to contain a limitation or future-work proposal. Generic finite-sample and imperfect-data warnings are not study-specific evidence.

Keep debugging notes, rejected interpretations and editorial uncertainty in project records unless they affect scientific interpretation. Preserve genuine negative findings, contrary evidence and material limitations. After removing defensive phrasing, reread the paragraph and its handoff: the author's judgment, support, necessary inference, conditions and references must still survive.

The conclusion answers the research question with the permitted claim, strongest evidence, unresolved boundary, and practical or scientific consequence. It need not promise generic future work.

## Final checks

- Abstract, title, highlights, figures, conclusion, and cover letter use mutually consistent claims.
- Every reported number and direction is traceable.
- Citations support the exact attached statement.
- Figures remain honest in color and grayscale, use interpretable scales, and match the text.
- Terminology, notation, units, dataset/split names, and method variants are consistent.
- Venue-specific limits and disclosures come from current official guidance, not this reference.
