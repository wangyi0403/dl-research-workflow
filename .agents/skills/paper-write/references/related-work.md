# Related Work and Comparison Positioning

Position the research among what the closest work actually establishes. Reuse the existing verified literature and claim records rather than creating a separate bibliography or state ledger.

Group work by a scientific question, capability, assumption, representation, data requirement or evaluation protocol. Explain the shared approach, what it enables and the boundary relevant to this paper. Chronology or paper-by-paper description is useful when a specific lineage matters; it is not the default synthesis structure.

Compare on exact dimensions: objective, assumptions, supervision, information access, computation, operating conditions, measurement or warranted claim. Support a claimed limitation with the source or a fair direct comparison. Credit useful prior capabilities; do not describe every prior study through the weakness this method happens to address.

Include foundational work, the closest alternatives and relevant recent or contrary evidence according to the question. Do not impose a fixed publication-age cutoff or citation quota. Distinguish predecessors, parallel work, reviews and primary evidence; deduplicate preprints and published versions.

Check whether the alleged gap is a new problem, a capability boundary, an untested assumption or merely missing evidence. Search failure is not proof of novelty. When abstracts cannot establish a contrast, mark the specific full-text check and draft only the supported comparison. Keep discovery uncertainty outside finished prose unless it is the actual scientific claim.
